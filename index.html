PFSS iso-B surface - PSP view

The PFSS source surface over the Parker Solar Probe mission, 2018–2026 — S. D. Bale, UC Berkeley / Imperial College

Each frame views the source surface from Parker Solar Probe's Carrington longitude and latitude, from launch in 2018 August through 2026 September. Time steps are adaptive: near perihelion PSP moves ∼200° in Carrington longitude in ∼1.5 days, so the encounters run in slow motion. Encounters 1–29 are labelled.

The surface is a PFSS solution driven by the ADAPT-GONG magnetogram nearest the middle of each Carrington rotation (CR2207–CR2315), with the outer boundary at 6 RS. Rather than a sphere we draw the surface of constant |B|, scaled to an area-weighted mean radius of 2.5 RS: it is elevated where the field is weak and depressed where it is strong. Colour is Br on that surface, fixed at ±0.193 G. Between rotations the surface is interpolated in time.

Measured polarities are mapped onto the surface: PSP footpoints (FIELDS, trailing 3 days), the 1 AU sector polarity from OMNI mapped back along the Parker spiral (white outline, trailing 27 days), and sign(BR) from Solar Orbiter since 2025 February (no outline, trailing one rotation), mapped at 375 km s−1.
